# Anti-slop email

Part of the Anti-Slop Kit by Social Mode. Use the anti-slop-writing skill for the rules on rhythm, phrases and specificity; this skill adds what's specific to the inbox.

People can smell a generated email from the first line, and the first line is usually "I hope this email finds you well." Write like a person who respects the reader's time and has a real reason to be in their inbox.

## Subject lines

- Specific and plain. "Your January menu launch: one idea" beats "Exciting opportunity!"
- No emoji, no clickbait, no ALL CAPS, no "Quick question" unless it really is one.
- Sentence case or lowercase reads more human than Title Case.

## Openings to delete

- "I hope this email finds you well" and every version of it.
- "I wanted to reach out", "I'm reaching out because", "Just circling back", "Just following up on my last email".
- "My name is X and I'm the Y at Z" as the first sentence. They can see who it's from.
- "I'd love to pick your brain" or "hop on a quick call".

Start with the reason you're writing, the thing you noticed about them, or the answer to their question.

## The body

- One email, one ask. Put the ask where it can't be missed, usually at the end of the first or second paragraph.
- Show you've done the work: reference something real and recent about the person or business. Never fake familiarity.
- Paragraphs of two to four sentences, varied in length. Not a stack of one-line paragraphs, and not one wall of text.
- Cut "Let me know if you have any questions" and "Please don't hesitate to reach out". They'll ask if they have questions.
- No bullet lists in a personal email unless you're laying out options or dates.
- No bold, no colours, no emoji in professional emails.

## Length

- Cold outreach: under 120 words.
- Follow-ups: two or three sentences, adding something new each time (a result, a date, a relevant article), never just "bumping this up".
- Client updates: lead with what's done and what you need from them, then the detail.

## Sign-offs

- Plain is fine: "Best", "Thanks", "Speak soon", or just your name.
- Avoid "Warmest regards", "Cheers to new beginnings" and anything with an emoji.
- Use a P.S. only when it adds something worth reading.

## Newsletters

- One main idea per issue, said in the subject line.
- Write to one reader, not "everyone".
- Your opinion is the product. Curation without a point of view is a link list.
- Keep design light: one image, readable type, one button at most.

## Replies

- Answer the question in the first line.
- Match the other person's length and formality.
- If you need to say no, say it early and kindly, with a reason and an alternative if there is one.

## Before sending

- Can the reader see why you're writing within two lines?
- Is there exactly one clear ask?
- Is there a stock opener, a stock closer or an emoji?
- Would this email still make sense if it were sent to a different person? If so, it isn't personal enough.
