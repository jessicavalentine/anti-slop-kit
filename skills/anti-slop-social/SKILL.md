# Anti-slop social posts

Part of the Anti-Slop Kit by Social Mode. Use the anti-slop-writing skill for every word on the slides and in the caption; this skill adds what's specific to social design.

People scroll past AI content because they've already seen it a thousand times. The fix isn't decoration. It's making choices a template would never make.

## The looks to avoid

These are the defaults AI tools fall into right now. Don't use them unless the brand already owns one of them:

- Warm cream background, elegant serif headline and a terracotta or burgundy accent.
- Near-black background with one neon accent colour.
- Purple-to-blue or pink-to-orange gradients.
- Frosted glass panels, soft glows and floating 3D blobs.
- Rounded cards in a neat grid, each with an icon, a bold title and two lines of text.
- Emoji used as icons or bullet points.
- Numbered markers (01, 02, 03) on things that aren't really a sequence.
- Every slide set in exactly the same layout, centred or left-aligned, start to finish.
- The fashionable "AI fonts" of the moment, chosen because they look expensive rather than for a reason. Whatever typefaces you pick, be able to say why they suit this brand.

## Covers

- The first slide does the stopping. Use a full-bleed image with the headline set over it, not a plain colour block with text.
- Real photography beats stock and beats AI renders. Tight crops, texture, real light, one subject.
- The headline names the idea in plain words. If it would work as the cover for a different brand, it's too generic.
- Credit images that aren't yours, on the slide or in the caption.

## Layout across a carousel

- Mix alignment deliberately: some slides left, some centred, some right. A carousel that never changes alignment looks generated.
- Alternate the rhythm: photo, then text, then photo-and-text, so each swipe feels like turning a magazine page.
- Break your own grid once per post on purpose: an oversized number, an image that bleeds off the edge, a headline set much bigger than the rest.
- One accent moment per slide at most. If everything is highlighted, nothing is.
- Keep consistent margins and a consistent header and footer so the breaks read as intentional.

## Type

- Never leave a widow: a single word stranded on the last line of a headline or paragraph. Rebalance the line breaks, tweak the wording, or adjust the size until every line carries at least two words.
- Two typefaces at most, three sizes at most per slide.
- Headlines are short and set large. Body copy is never more than three sentences per slide.
- Labels in small capitals get extra letter-spacing; long text never does.

## Structure of a carousel

1. Cover: the hook, over a full-bleed image.
2. The moment: what happened, with names, places and dates.
3. The detail that proves it: a number, a quote, a photo.
4. The bigger picture: is this a one-off or a direction?
5. The read: your opinion, stated plainly.
6. The close: one question or one call to action.

Adjust the count to the story. Seven slides is a guide, not a rule.

## Captions

- The first line is a full thought that stands on its own. It appears before "more", so it has to earn the tap.
- Write in paragraphs that sound like a person, not a list of benefits with emoji in front.
- One call to action. A question you actually want answered beats "Drop a 🔥 below".
- Three to five hashtags, chosen for discovery. No hashtag walls.
- Tag the people and brands involved. Disclose paid partnerships, gifting and any client relationship.
- Never write fake reviews, fake quotes or invented statistics.

## Bios

- Say what you do and who it's for in fewer than 150 characters.
- Mystery is fine; vagueness isn't. A reader should know whether you're for them.

## Before posting

- Does the cover stop the scroll without the caption?
- Is there a widow anywhere?
- Does alignment change across the carousel?
- Would this slide work for a competitor with the logo swapped? If so, rewrite it.
- Is every fact checked and every image credited?
