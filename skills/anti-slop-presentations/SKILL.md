# Anti-slop presentations

Part of the Anti-Slop Kit by Social Mode. Use the anti-slop-writing skill for all the words, including speaker notes.

An AI deck is easy to spot: an agenda slide, a gradient title, three rounded cards with icons on every second slide, bullet points that say nothing, and a "Thank you! Questions?" at the end. A good deck makes one argument, one slide at a time.

## Start with the argument

- Write the whole story as slide titles first, before designing anything. Read the titles in order: they should make the full argument on their own.
- Every slide title is a sentence that states its takeaway. "Footfall doubled after the pop-up" beats "Results".
- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two titles, it's two slides.

## Slides to cut

- Agenda slides for decks under twenty slides.
- "About us" slides that come before the client's problem.
- "Thank you" and "Questions?" slides. End on the ask, the next step or the strongest image.
- Slides that only exist to look busy: icon grids, vague timelines, stock photos of handshakes.

## Design

- Avoid the default AI looks: gradient title slides, glass panels, neon on black, cream and serif with a terracotta accent, and three rounded cards with an icon each.
- Vary the layouts: full-bleed image, big number, quote, chart, single statement. Don't repeat the same layout three slides running.
- Mix alignment on purpose rather than centring or left-aligning everything.
- No widows in titles or body copy.
- Use real images: the client's product, place, people and work. Avoid stock and AI renders unless they're labelled as concepts.
- Two typefaces, a strict type scale and generous margins. Leave white space; it signals confidence.
- Numbered markers only for real sequences (steps, phases, dates).

## Words on slides

- Fewer than thirty words on most slides. The talking goes in the speaker notes.
- No bullet lists of three vague benefits. If you need a list, make every item specific.
- Numbers need a source, a date and a comparison ("up 38% on last November", not "huge growth").
- Name names: the client, the market, the competitor, the place.

## Charts

- One message per chart, written as the slide title.
- Label the data directly instead of using a legend.
- Highlight the one bar or line that matters and mute the rest.
- Start axes at zero for bar charts. Never fake precision.

## Proposals and pitches

- Open with the client's situation in their words, then your read of it, then what you'd do.
- Show thinking, not just services: one example of how you'd approach their real problem beats a list of offerings.
- Put pricing and next steps on clear slides of their own.
- Keep company legal names and invoice details out of client-facing decks unless they're needed for the contract.

## Before sending

- Read only the slide titles in order: do they tell the story?
- Is there a slide that could go in any other company's deck unchanged?
- Is every number sourced and every image credited or owned?
- Is there a widow, a gradient title or an icon grid?
