# Anti-slop writing

The core of the Anti-Slop Kit by Social Mode. The social, presentation and email skills all build on this one, so read it first whenever you write anything people will see.

AI copy gives itself away through evenness. Every sentence is the same length, every paragraph has the same shape, every list has three items and every claim could belong to any brand on earth. Your job is to write the opposite: uneven in the right places, specific everywhere, and recognisably from one person with a point of view.

## Two modes

**Write or edit (default).** Produce the copy, then add a short "What I changed" note when editing someone else's draft.

**Audit.** When asked whether something reads as AI, or to check it without rewriting, list each pattern below that appears, quote the line, and suggest a fix in a few words. Don't rewrite, don't give a score and don't guess whether a machine wrote it. Patterns are evidence; detectors are guesses.

Before writing, know three things: who is reading, where it will appear, and what they should do or feel afterwards. If any of those is missing and you can't infer it, ask one question.

## Rhythm

- Vary sentence length on purpose. Let one sentence run long and loose, carrying a thought all the way through, and then stop with a short one.
- Don't stack short punchy fragments. "Bold. Brave. Unapologetic." is the single most recognisable AI rhythm.
- Paragraphs should differ in length too. A one-line paragraph is a tool, not a habit.
- Read it aloud. If you'd never say it to a person across a table, rewrite it.

## Lists of three

Models reach for three by default: three adjectives, three clauses, three benefits. Use two, or one good one, or four if the content really has four. Keep a three only when the subject actually has three parts.

## Phrases that mark copy as generated

Cut these, or rewrite the sentence so it doesn't need them:

- Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era of", "Picture this", "Imagine a world where", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "Buckle up".
- Fake depth: "It's not just X, it's Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y", "More than just a", "The real question is", "What nobody tells you".
- Colon reveals: "The secret? Consistency." "The result: magic."
- Rhetorical questions used as hooks: "Ever wondered why...?" "What if I told you...?"
- Closers: "Let that sink in", "The future is here", "And that's the magic", "Food for thought", "The choice is yours".
- Words: delve, elevate, unlock, unleash, seamless, curated, elevated, game-changer, next-level, tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, journey (for anything that isn't a trip), navigate (for anything that isn't a map), embark, foster, leverage, harness, robust, holistic, synergy, bespoke (unless it's tailoring), vibrant, nestled, boasts, whether you're X or Y.
- Empty intensifiers: truly, really, incredibly, absolutely, genuinely, honestly, literally. Keep one only if it carries real emphasis.

## Punctuation tells

- Em dashes used for dramatic asides in every paragraph. Use commas, full stops or brackets instead, and keep dashes rare.
- Scare quotes around invented labels ("the 'quiet luxury' effect").
- Exclamation marks in professional copy.
- Title Case Headings On Everything. Use sentence case.
- Emoji as bullet points or section markers.

## Specificity

- Names, numbers, places and dates beat adjectives. "Footfall rose 38% in the first fortnight" beats "a huge success".
- The swap test: if you could replace the brand, city or person with another and the sentence still works, it says nothing. Rewrite it with a detail only this subject has.
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or testimonials to make copy sound specific. If a detail is missing, leave a clear placeholder like [number] or ask.
- Show the thing instead of announcing that it's important, exciting or game-changing.

## Point of view

- Take a position. Generated copy hedges everything and offends no one, which is why it's forgettable.
- Keep the writer's own quirks when editing: their slang, their bluntness, their jokes, their spelling (British or American), the odd long tangent that's clearly theirs. Make the minimum edit that removes the slop.
- Use active verbs and real subjects. "We opened the store on Tuesday" beats "the launch was facilitated".

## Formatting

- Don't use headers, bullets or bold on something short enough to read in one breath.
- Don't bold random phrases for emphasis. If everything is bold, nothing is.
- Don't end with a summary of what you just said, or a "Conclusion" section.

## Final pass

Before handing anything over, check:

1. Does the first line earn the second?
2. Is there a list of three that doesn't need to be three?
3. Are any two sentences in a row the same length and shape?
4. Does any sentence pass the swap test?
5. Is there a banned phrase, an emoji bullet or a dramatic em dash?
6. Would the writer recognise this as their own voice?
